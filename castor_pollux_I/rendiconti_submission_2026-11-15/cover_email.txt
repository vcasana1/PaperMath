Subject: Submission to Rendiconti di Matematica e delle sue Applicazioni: An elementary-arithmetic splitting of the log-sine Mellin transform

Dear Editors,

I would like to submit the enclosed manuscript, "An elementary-arithmetic splitting of the log-sine Mellin transform", for publication as a research article in Rendiconti di Matematica e delle sue Applicazioni.

The paper contains three results that I would like to bring to your attention.

1. A new constant, analogous to Euler's constant. The paper introduces

   C_g = - int_0^1 log sin(pi {1/v}) dv = 0.7437948618...,

where {.} is the fractional part. It is the log-sine analogue of the classical formula 1 - gamma = int_0^1 {1/v} dv for Euler's constant: the fractional part {1/v} is replaced by -log sin(pi {1/v}). The constant has a closed expression in terms of the values zeta(2k), and its decimal expansion has been allocated the number A402034 in the OEIS.

2. A limit formula at the primes. For the product P(x) of the numbers sin(pi x/k) over the integers 2 <= k < x, which is zero exactly at the composite integers, the paper proves that

   |P(p)|^(1/p) -> e^(-C_g) as p -> infinity through the primes.

So C_g is the exponential rate of this product along the primes.

3. A new kind of decomposition for the zeta function: an arithmetic part and an elementary part. The proof of the limit leads to two functions of s, both Mellin integrals of h(u) = log|2 sin(pi u)|: the arithmetic part G(s), taken over u > 1, which contains every singularity of h at the integers and is entire, and the elementary part A(s), taken over the single period 0 < u < 1, which is meromorphic with an explicit Mittag-Leffler expansion whose coefficients are zeta(2k)/k. They satisfy

   G(s) - A(s) = (pi/s) cot(pi s/2) zeta(s),

so that a non-real s is a zero of zeta if and only if G(s) = A(s), and C_g = log 2 - G(1). The zeros are thus described by the equality of two functions of different nature, which can be studied separately.

The paper then studies this identification: a non-negative function Q with Q'' = -h whose Mellin transform is the sum of three terms, one given by G and two, which cancel to two orders, given by A; and deformations of h that keep this non-negative function while placing the points where the deformed parts coincide on prescribed vertical lines.

I believe the paper may interest readers working on the zeta function, on log-sine integrals and on mathematical constants.
