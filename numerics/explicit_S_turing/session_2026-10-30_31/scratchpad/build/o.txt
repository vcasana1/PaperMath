Introduction

The imaginary parts γk of the nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function are located only on
average by the Riemann–von Mangoldt formula: the points τk defined by N0 (τk ) = k − 12 (1.2),
where N0 is the smooth main term of the zero-counting function, discard the oscillating term
S(t) = π1 arg ζ( 21 + it). The primes enter through S. The Euler product writes ζ(s) as a product
of one factor (1 − p−s )−1 for each prime p, so that S(t) is, formally,
√ minus the sum over p of

S in mean square, unconditionally (Selberg, Tsang, Fujii; see below). The arithmetic correction
studied here adds this finite prime sum, Φ in (1.10), to the main term: the zeros are then
approximated by the points τkC of the equation N0 (t) + Φ(t) = k − 21 instead of N0 (t) = k − 21 .
The main result constructs the strictly increasing sequence τkC through the sublevel measures
(1.12) of N0 + Φ, where Φ is defined by finitely many Euler phases. Unconditionally, the rootmean-square error of τkC with respect to γk on dyadic blocks is O(1/ log T ), a bounded multiple
of the mean zero spacing (Theorem 1.5). The key technical feature is the sublevel-set definition:
it remains valid even when the prime-corrected counting function N0 +Φ is not monotone, where
the equation N0 + Φ = k − 12 may have no unique root.

coming from Selberg’s mean square of S. Thus the arithmetic correction removes the factor
log log T from the proved mean-square upper order. Further consequences proved below describe
the arithmetic correction itself and the defect of the ordinal equation when evaluated at the
actual imaginary parts γk .

reference to [40, Thm. 5.1].

Conclusion

The principal conclusion is the unconditional, fully ordinally indexed construction τkC from
finitely many Euler phases. The sublevel measures remain strictly increasing even though the
underlying prime-complemented counting function need not be monotone. Theorem 1.5 proves
a root-mean-square localization error O(1/ log T ) over the complete list of zeros, including multiplicities.
The exact defect identity provides a separate unconditional asymptotic: the uncorrected
Riemann–von Mangoldt points τk have mean-square error 2 log log T / log2 T (1 + o(1)). The
arithmetic correction removes the divergent factor. Proposition 9.1 shows that the correction
has the same leading second moment and is asymptotically aligned with the actual displacement
vector.
Theorem 9.3 and Corollary 9.4 demonstrate the related bounded ordinal defect and the
leading second-moment asymptotic of the prime phases at the imaginary parts.
Independently, the argument estimates yield lim supk |τk − γk | ≤ 0.60832 and the explicit
bound |S(T )| ≤ 0.09783 log T +0.7214 log log T +10.6 for T ≥ e. The improved leading coefficient
comes from pointwise reflection by the functional equation on the left of the critical line. As
explained in Section 1.2, it improves the complete estimate of Bellotti–Wong only beyond the
stated crossing height.
Theorem 9.5 gives a deterministic criterion for certifying simple critical-line zeros from individual ordinal defects. The available bounds do not satisfy the numerical threshold required to
deduce RH, simplicity, or any stated positive proportion from this certificate.
Limitations of this work. The constant in Theorem 1.5 is not explicit, so the result gives
no numerical guarantee at any finite height; it controls the mean square, not every individual
imaginary part, and the explicit bound of Theorem 1.3 improves on Bellotti–Wong only for
log T > 2425.84, as it trades the constant term for the leading coefficient (for comparison, (1.8)
30
