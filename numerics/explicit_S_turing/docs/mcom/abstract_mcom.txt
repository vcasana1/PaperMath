ABSTRACT (LaTeX, for the submission form; 247 words)

Let $N(T)$ be the number of non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta-function with imaginary part in $(0,T]$, counted with multiplicity, and let $S(T)$ be the argument of $\zeta$ on the critical line, normalised so that $N(T)=\frac1\pi\vartheta(T)+1+S(T)$. Two estimates are proved by the method of Turing type that Amberger introduced for Dedekind zeta-functions and that Bellotti and Wong extended to Artin $L$-functions. The first is explicit: for every $T\ge1$, $|N(T)-\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi e}|\le 0.08382\log T+5.326$ and $|S(T)|\le 0.08382\log T+4.444$, and a piecewise linear bound, formed by six lines, lies below the explicit bounds of Amberger and of Bellotti and Wong for every $T\ge1$. The kernels of the method are found by convex optimisation instead of by hand, and the constant coming from the Euler product is evaluated rigorously in ball arithmetic over all prime powers up to $10^8$. The second estimate is asymptotic: $\limsup_{T\to\infty}|S(T)|/\log T<0.0748596142711$. It comes from a hybrid of the kernel majorant with Jensen's formula on discs to the right of the critical line, where the growth of $\zeta$ is controlled by the subconvexity bounds of Bourgain, of Trudgian and Yang, of Tao, Trudgian and Yang, and of Heath-Brown. The constant is smaller than the value $0.0786252\ldots$ below which the method of Backlund cannot go, even under the Lindel\"of hypothesis, for the family of discs centred near $s=1$ considered in the author's earlier comparison. Every numerical inequality involving the chosen kernels and constants is certified in ball arithmetic, and no numerical information on the zeros is used.
